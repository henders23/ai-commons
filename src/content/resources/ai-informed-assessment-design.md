## What you get

A working draft of around 60,000 words, organised so that every chapter stands alone.

- **Part I, the landscape.** The assessment we inherited, what AI actually does and does not do, and the challenges, risks and opportunities.
- **Part II, foundations.** Starting with learning outcomes, rethinking the validity question, and the spectrum from AI-excluded to AI-integrated assessment that organises the rest of the book.
- **Part III, AI-resilient assessment.** Assessing what AI cannot do, process not just product, authentic and contextualised assessment, oral and live assessment.
- **Part IV, AI-integrated assessment.** AI as a tool within the assessment, using AI to generate better feedback, AI-assisted marking and moderation.
- **Part V, implementation.** Auditing and redesigning your current assessments, academic integrity reimagined, equity and inclusion, working within your institution.
- **Part VI, looking forward.** The educator's evolving role, and designing for a moving target.
- **Four appendices.** An assessment audit tool and redesign template, a glossary of AI terms for educators, an annotated bibliography, and sample assessment briefs across disciplines.

Four recurring features run through the chapters: *In this chapter* previews, *In practice* worked examples across disciplines, *Try this* protocols and checklists, and *What this means for you*, which closes each chapter with guidance for each of the three readers.

## How to use it

**With an immediate redesign problem.** Start at Chapter 14, the audit and redesign workflow, and work backwards. Appendix A is the ready-to-use version of that workflow: an audit record for every summative task, a programme coverage check, a redesign plan per task, and an equity review.

**Responsible for policy.** Begin with Chapters 3, 15 and 17: the stakes, integrity reimagined, and working within your institution.

**With your team.** Chapter 6, the spectrum from AI-excluded to AI-integrated, gives a team a shared vocabulary for talking about every task on a course. Run the Appendix A audit together on one module before deciding anything.

## Why it matters for EAP

EAP centres assess writing, and are often asked to advise the disciplines on it. The book's central move, relocating the evidence of learning to what AI cannot supply and treating detection as a dead end, is the same argument the *Authorship in the Age of AI* paper makes from an academic literacies perspective. This book is the practical, institution-wide companion to that paper.

## A note on currency

Where the book states that AI can or cannot do something, the statement is true of the systems available in 2026 and should be treated as provisional. Chapter 19 explains how to design for that uncertainty.
